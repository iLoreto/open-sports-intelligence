How We Read the Game

The scoreboard tells you what happened. Watching the game helps you understand what might happen next. The market price tells you whether that possibility is worth the risk.

We're developing a human-led research method for live sports markets. Not a magic engine, not automatic bets, and certainly not guaranteed winners.

Four sports. Different kinds of uncertainty.

🎾 Tennis

Watch the individual: serve percentage, return pressure, footwork, errors and fatigue. A player can struggle before the scoreboard reveals it—but they can recover too.

Research concept: Collapse Indicator

🏀 Basketball

Watch the five-player unit: spacing, cohesion, rotations and who gets minutes. A scoring prop depends on both pace and opportunity.

Research concept: Pace & Minutes

🏒 Hockey

The puck moves fast. A deflection, penalty or turnover can turn a controlled game into a scramble. Shot quality matters more than raw volume alone.

Research concept: Storm Risk

⚽ Soccer

A huge field hides off-ball movement. One counterattack, red card or penalty can transform a match. Possession does not guarantee safety.

Research concept: Storm Risk

🥊 UFC: a specialized combat-sports category

Separate analytical discipline; implementation pending. UFC and other MMA events require a dedicated combat-sports research framework, not a copy of team-sport scoring, pace or momentum indicators. The analytical rules, code, tests and validation for this category have not yet been written.

Fight outcomes can change in a single exchange, submission or stoppage, even when one fighter appears to be ahead on accumulated statistics. A future framework may examine striking and defensive efficiency, grappling, takedown defense, cardio, age, reach, weight-class changes, opponent quality, round dynamics and finishing risk. These are candidate research factors, not verified predictive signals.

UFC policy: research and comparison only; no betting recommendations. This category will not publish fight picks, recommended wagers, stakes, betting confidence percentages or automated ADD/HOLD/REDUCE/EXIT trading signals. The goal is to help visitors compare fighters and understand the evidence without encouraging financial risk.

Future tools, not yet implemented: Side-by-side fighter profiles, records, striking and grappling comparisons, defensive metrics, weight-class history, opponent quality and relevant fight context. The expert-led comparison and interpretation process will be documented by the project owner before any analytical engine is implemented. Until then, do not present proposed comparisons or conclusions as live functionality. All statistics must have identifiable sources and limitations; no metric guarantees a fight outcome.

Calm water is not always safe water

Soccer and hockey can turn like the ocean. A team may look comfortable until a sudden high-impact play changes everything. Our proposed Storm Risk framework asks whether the conditions supporting a lead are deteriorating—not merely who is ahead.

The number of players involved is a useful way to think about complexity, but it does not prove one sport is inherently easier or safer to predict. Broadcast visibility, scoring frequency, data quality and market price all matter.

Case study: Brunson's 25-point pace

During a Knicks–Bucks game, we followed Jalen Brunson's reported scoring as the game developed. This is an illustrative, user-reported sequence, not a verified historical feed.

At 21 points through 36 elapsed game minutes, a simple straight-line extrapolation suggested 28 points by the end of regulation. But that estimate ignored substitutions, actual minutes played and defensive changes. The observer also noticed Milwaukee cutting into New York's lead, potentially increasing the need to keep Brunson playing.

The lesson isn't “always enter early.” An earlier entry may pay more because it carries more uncertainty. The question is whether new information justifies the price and the additional exposure.

Player props: excluded across all sports

Standing research and recommendation policy: Open Sports Intelligence does not recommend individual-player performance props in any sport or league. This supersedes the previous NBA-specific exclusion and all earlier points-threshold rules. No exceptions are made for star players, favorable matchups or attractive quoted prices.

The exclusion covers individual points, rebounds, assists, three-pointers, goals, shots, passing, rushing, receiving, hits, strikeouts, tennis player statistics, fighter-specific performance and other individual performance thresholds, whether offered alone or as legs of a combined ticket. Research may still use player statistics to assess team- or game-level outcomes, but must not turn them into player-prop recommendations.

Why this policy? Individual outcomes can be affected by playing time, substitutions, injuries, fouls, tactical changes, blowouts and other conditions outside a player's performance ability. Our analyst identified repeated unfavorable outcomes in personal experience and has chosen to avoid this entire market category. This is a risk-tolerance decision, not a statistically established claim that all player props have low win probabilities or that participants manipulate outcomes.

Eligible research instead: Team moneylines, team totals, full-game totals and other non-player markets, subject to sport-specific evidence, market pricing, liquidity and predefined exposure limits. These markets still carry substantial risk and are not guaranteed safer or profitable.

Implementation status: This is the public editorial recommendation policy. Scanner-level exclusion filters require separate implementation and verification; do not assume they are deployed.

The 10× Discipline Rule: feel the weight before committing

Mental resistance training, not financial accounting: Before placing a trade, imagine the stake is ten times larger. Treat a $10 commitment with the seriousness of $100, $50 like $500, and $100 like $1,000. This is a voluntary reflection exercise to make risk harder to dismiss—not a statement that the actual financial loss is ten times greater.

Like an athlete using resistance in training, the goal is to build deliberate habits under controlled conditions. The imagined extra zero belongs before the decision. Afterward, record actual dollars gained, lost or still exposed. Do not multiply losses mentally, use the exercise to shame yourself, or chase money to erase an imagined debt.

Pre-order safeguard: Independently verify the event, market, selections, stake, potential payout, existing open positions and total correlated exposure before confirming. Check for duplicate tickets. If any detail is uncertain, do not submit the order.

Stop rule: If a mistake, loss or emotional reaction creates an urge to win money back immediately, stop placing orders for the session. Do not increase stakes, make new deposits or add correlated positions to recover losses. Protect available funds and review the decision later using actual account figures.

Public takeaway: Train risk perception at 10×; keep accounting at 1×. This is a personal discipline framework, not a proven method for improving betting returns or a reason to trade.

The Attention and Exposure Rule

Do not open more live positions than you can responsibly understand, monitor and manage. Your attention is limited, and more simultaneous games can mean missed changes, correlated exposure and avoidable stress.

Before entering, check how many games and positions are already active, whether their outcomes overlap, your total cash at risk, and whether you could still make a calm decision if several games changed at once. Watching a game can add context, but it does not guarantee an edge, and constant monitoring is not required when no useful action is available.

Parlays are not an exception to risk limits. A small-stake, 12-leg parlay can be watched for entertainment, but each required leg compounds the chance that the ticket fails. For illustration, if 12 legs were independent and each had a 70% chance of winning, all 12 would win only about 1.38% of the time (0.7012). Real legs may be correlated, so do not treat this illustration as an estimate for a specific ticket.

The Game-First Monitoring Rule

Do not bite off more than you can chew. Financial capacity is not the same as monitoring capacity. Each live position requires enough attention to recognize material changes and respond responsibly; unused bankroll is not a reason to open another position.

Once a position is established, close the betting app. Follow the game through the broadcast and an independent sports-statistics source such as ESPN or UFC event statistics, where available. Monitor sport-specific evidence such as pace, minutes, rotations, possession, shot quality, injuries, fatigue or round-by-round performance rather than repeatedly watching the position's dollar value.

Evidence first, market second: Define warning signs and exit deadlines before entry. If a meaningful statistical or visual warning appears, or a predetermined review/exit time arrives, reopen the betting app to check the executable price and liquidity. A warning prompts reassessment, not an automatic exit. Choose HOLD, REDUCE or EXIT deliberately; ADD is considered only if the evidence supports it and total exposure remains within the preset cap. Then close the betting app again.

Monitoring safeguards: Broadcast and statistical feeds can lag, and exits may be unavailable or costly. Do not rely on alerts alone for time-sensitive positions. If you cannot responsibly follow multiple games or act within the required window, reduce the number of open positions or refrain from opening new ones.

The Disengagement Rule

When no useful action remains, close the betting app and step away. Once positions are committed, repeatedly checking prices can increase stress without improving the outcome. Do not add bets just to feel in control, chase a loss, or keep watching a market. Settle and review the actual results later, when you can assess them calmly.

The Independent Decision Rule

Never copy someone else's stake or confidence without independently checking the reasoning, price and your own risk. Another bettor may have a much larger bankroll, unseen hedges, a different entry price, or an incomplete public track record. A winning streak does not guarantee future results.

Our decision discipline

ADD

Evidence strengthens the thesis, the price offers value, and total exposure stays within a predefined limit.

HOLD

The original thesis remains supported; no compelling new reason to change.

REDUCE

New warnings, deteriorating conditions or excessive exposure justify cutting risk.

EXIT

The thesis materially breaks and an executable exit is preferable to remaining exposed.

These are research labels for human decisions—not trade signals, instructions or automated execution.

What we're building next

We want the radar to combine sport-specific observations, reliable source timestamps, transparent scoring projections, estimated fair value, and position-risk awareness. We will test ideas against historical data before treating them as predictive indicators.

What exists today versus what's proposed: The current workbench supports manual market scanning and limited sport research. The Collapse Indicator, Storm Risk, live player-prop projections and decision labels described here are proposed capabilities, not features already deployed.

Our mission: Software should make people's lives better, not worse. That includes being clear about uncertainty and never claiming certainty where there is none.
